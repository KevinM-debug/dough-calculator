Swap almond flour for coconut flour

Coconut flour soaks up about four times as much liquid, so it's never a 1:1 swap. This is a starting point; let the batter rest 5 minutes and add a spoon of water if it's stiff.

Why keto bread fails

  1. Gummy, wet middle: underbaked, or cut warm. Bake until the bottom sounds hollow (or 200°F inside), then cool completely. Keto bread sets as it cools.
  2. Dense and flat: not enough binder. Psyllium powder (not whole husks) plus egg whites holds the air. Check the baking powder is fresh: a spoon in hot water should fizz hard.
  3. Crumbly, falls apart: too little psyllium or egg, or too much coconut flour.
  4. Brick-hard: coconut flour swapped 1:1 for almond flour. Use the swap helper above.
  5. Tastes eggy: too many yolks. Use egg whites and a splash of vinegar.
  6. Purple loaf: some psyllium brands turn purple. Harmless; a lighter brand avoids it.
  7. Collapsed after baking: too much baking powder or the oven door opened early.
  8. Want it to taste like real bread? The wheat-gluten loaf is the closest: it rises with yeast and toasts like bread.

Chaffle problems: soggy = not hot enough or opened too early (wait until the steam stops); stuck = iron not fully preheated (sprinkle a little cheese on the iron first); raw middle with a dark outside = too much batter for the iron; eggy = more cheese, a spoon of almond flour.

Bringing recipes over from Paprika

Your whole collection (best on the Mac):

  1. In Paprika, select the recipes you want (Command-A selects all).
  2. Choose File → Export and pick Paprika Recipe Format. You get a file ending in .paprikarecipes.
  3. Send that file to the device you'll cook with (AirDrop, iCloud Drive, or email).
  4. Here, tap Import from Paprika and choose the file. Recipes that are already here get skipped.

One recipe at a time: open it in Paprika, use Share to copy it as text, then tap Paste a recipe here.

Stretching practice

Snap a photo of each pizza you stretch and rate it. Over a few weeks you'll see the shapes get rounder and the rims more even.

Stretching tips ›
  1. Room-temperature dough. Cold dough fights back and tears. Give fridge dough its full warm-up; it should feel soft and puffy.
  2. Flour both sides well (semolina or flour), and shake off the extra before it goes on the peel.
  3. Press, don't pull, at first. With your fingertips, press from the center outward, leaving a 1 cm band untouched all around. That band becomes the rim.
  4. Turn and press. Rotate the dough a quarter turn between presses so it stays round.
  5. Knuckle stretch. Drape it over the backs of both hands (fists, not fingertips) and rotate, letting gravity do the work. Keep your hands under the rim, not the middle.
  6. Thin spot or tear? Pinch it closed, or push dough in from the thicker parts. Holes usually mean the dough is too cold, under-rested, or stretched in the middle.
  7. Springs back? Stop, cover it, and let it rest 10 minutes. The gluten relaxes and it stretches easily after.
  8. No rolling pin for Neapolitan or New York: it presses the air out of the rim.

Sync & backup

Everything lives on this device only. To move it to another phone or computer, send a sync file and open it there. Opening a sync file merges it: new things get added, newer edits win, and anything you deleted stays deleted.

2. Open it on the other device

How to

iPhone or iPad → Mac: tap Send sync file, choose AirDrop and your Mac. On the Mac, open this site, tap Open sync file, and pick it from Downloads.

Mac → iPhone: click Save file, AirDrop it to the phone and choose Save to Files. On the phone, open this site, tap Open sync file, and pick it in Files.

To the girls (Messages, WhatsApp, email): send the file. On iPhone, tap it, then the share button, then Save to Files. On Android it lands in Downloads. Then open this site and tap Open sync file.

Just one formula or recipe? Use Send formula link on the dough page, or Send as link on a recipe. Whoever taps the link gets it in their app.
